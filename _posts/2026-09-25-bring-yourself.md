A senior colleague who mentors me gave me two pieces of advice recently:

> Always keep your passion, and stay positive.
>
> Whatever you're working on, have your own concluded opinion.

On paper, the first reads like a poster in a dentist's waiting room and the second like a line from a performance review. I agreed with both anyway. And the longer I sit with them, the more they look like one piece of advice said twice.

---

Passion first, since it's the one that sounds soft.

[Back in March](/blog/rediscovering-curiosity) I wrote about losing my curiosity for a decade without noticing. Passion leaves the same way. No burnout, no dramatic exit. You just get experienced. You've watched enough projects stall that you can list, from memory, the reasons the next one will stall too, and after a while that list starts to feel like wisdom.

Mostly it's just cheap. Plenty of new things do fail, so predicting failure makes you right often enough to feel smart.

Staying positive is the expensive move. You see every reason it won't work, and you go looking for the one way it might anyway. That takes more effort than the list does.

Also, look at the verb. Keep. Mid-career, nobody tells you to go find your passion. They tell you to keep it, because the default is losing it.

---

The second one is more concrete. When you bring someone a problem, bring your answer with it.

Two versions of the same message:

> The release is blocked on a flaky test. We could roll back, wait for the fix, or ship behind a flag. Thoughts?

> The release is blocked on a flaky test. I think we ship behind a flag: the fix is two days out, and a rollback costs more than it saves. Doing that tomorrow morning unless someone objects.

The first one looks humble and collaborative. It also hands the hardest part of the problem, the deciding, to whoever reads it. The second one doesn't even need a reply.

My mentor's reason was the part that stuck with me: walk in with the mindset that you're the one solving it. Without a conclusion, you're a router. The problem passed through you and moved one hop closer to whoever ends up deciding.

There's a quieter reason people send the first message, too. A menu can't be wrong. If the flag goes badly, it wasn't your call.

Concluding has a real downside, and I have recent experience with it. In July I published a post titled "A World Champion Who Doesn't Exist." The win was real. Someone pointed that out two hours later, and I [rewrote the post](/blog/i-checked-the-wrong-table) that same afternoon.

That only happened because I'd said something clear enough to be wrong. "Might be real, might not be" would have just sat there. There's nothing in a hedge to push against.

So "concluded" doesn't mean certain. It means you did the work, then said out loud where you landed. What went wrong in July was a conclusion with one lookup behind it. The fix for that is more work, not fewer conclusions.

---

Put the two side by side and each one covers the other's failure mode.

Passion without a conclusion is cheerleading: lots of energy, no direction. A conclusion without passion is the cynic's specialty. "That won't work" is about as concluded as an opinion gets. It's just delivered from the sidelines.

Each one alone gives you a type everyone has worked with. Together they say one thing: bring your energy and your judgment. Bring yourself.

They feed each other, too. Working a problem all the way to a conclusion is tiring, and what keeps you going is caring how it turns out. Then once your conclusion is on the table, you care a lot more.

That's my concluded opinion, anyway. If it's wrong, someone will tell me. Historically, within about two hours.

最近，一位比我 senior 的同事、也是我的 mentor，给了我两条建议：

> 永远要保持 passion，保持积极的心态。
>
> 工作也好，处理事情也好，要有自己的 concluded opinion。

单看字面，第一条像贴在牙科诊所候诊室里的励志海报，第二条像绩效评语里的一句话。可我两条都认。而且越琢磨越觉得，这俩其实是同一条建议，说了两遍罢了。

---

先说 passion，它听上去最虚。

三月我写过一篇[《重拾好奇心》](/blog/rediscovering-curiosity)，讲自己怎么在十年里不知不觉把好奇心弄丢了。热情也是这么丢的。没有 burnout，也没什么戏剧性的时刻，就是人慢慢变"有经验"了。项目黄了一个又一个，下一个刚冒头，你张嘴就能数出一串它会黄的理由。数多了，还挺像智慧的。

其实也没那么高级吧。新东西本来就经常失败，唱衰十次里总能蒙对几次，足够显得自己很聪明了。

保持积极反而是贵的那个选项：不行的理由你全看见了，还愿意再去找那一条"万一能行"的路。这比列清单费劲多了。

还有，注意那个动词："保持"。到了这个阶段，已经没人劝你去找热情了，大家只劝你保持住。因为放着不管，默认结局就是丢。

---

第二条更具体：把问题带给别人的时候，把答案也一起带上。

同一件事，两种发法：

> 发布卡在一个 flaky test 上。可以回滚，可以等修复，也可以先用 feature flag 关着上线。大家怎么看？

> 发布卡在一个 flaky test 上。我建议先用 flag 关着上线：修复还要两天，回滚的代价比收益大。明早就这么办，有意见现在提。

第一条看着谦虚，也很有协作精神。可它把整件事里最难的那一步，也就是拍板，原封不动地甩给了读消息的人。第二条呢？连回复都不用。

我这位 mentor 给的理由是：要带着自己来解决问题的心态。这句我最认同。没有结论，你就只是个转发器。问题在你手里过了一道，原样递给了下一个人，离最后拍板的那位又近了一步而已。

人爱发第一种消息，还有个不太说得出口的原因：菜单是不会错的。flag 出问题了？又不是我拍的板嘛。

下结论当然有风险，这学费我最近刚交过。七月我发过一篇文章，标题叫《那个不存在的世界冠军》。结果那场胜利是真的。发出去两个小时就有人指出来，当天下午我就把文章[重写了](/blog/i-checked-the-wrong-table)。

能被这么快纠正，恰恰因为我那句话够明确，明确到能被证明是错的。要是我当时写的是"可能是真的，也可能是假的"，这话就会一直挂在那儿，谁也没法反驳。

所以 concluded 不等于"确定"。它的意思是功课做完了，再把自己的落点说出来。七月那次的毛病，是结论背后只有一次检索。该补的是功课，不是少下结论。

---

两条放一起看，刚好互相补对方的短板。

光有热情没有结论，是啦啦队：劲儿很足，就是没方向。光有结论没有热情，是看客的拿手戏。"这事成不了"大概是世界上最 concluded 的 opinion 了，只不过是站在场外说的。曾国藩那句话我[四月引过](/blog/hype-trends-and-the-inner-anchor)：在局外呐喊议论，总是无益。

两条合起来，其实就一句话：热情和判断，都带上。说白了，就是把自己带上。

而且这俩是互相养着的。把一个问题一路想到能下结论，挺累的，能撑着人想下去的，只有在乎。结论一旦摆上桌，你只会更在乎。

这就是我的 concluded opinion。错了的话，会有人告诉我的。按以往的记录，大概两个小时吧。
